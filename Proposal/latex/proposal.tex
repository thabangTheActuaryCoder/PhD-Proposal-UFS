\begin{abstract}
Valuing the guarantees embedded in variable annuities is computationally expensive because the liability has no closed form and must be estimated by Monte Carlo simulation across many contracts, scenarios and reporting dates. Neural network emulators can approximate this valuation at a fraction of the cost, but existing emulators are trained on values alone and discard a resource that the same simulation already produces almost for free, namely the sensitivities of the value to the underlying risk factors. This proposal develops a sensitivity augmented deep learning framework for variable annuity guarantee valuation under stochastic mortality. The emulator is trained jointly on Monte Carlo values and their pathwise sensitivities, so that it learns the valuation surface and its slopes together. The central claims are, first, that this augmentation raises accuracy and reduces the number of simulated training points required, and second, that it yields accurate risk sensitivities for hedging as a direct by product. In parallel, the framework attaches calibrated uncertainty to every prediction, separating the Monte Carlo noise of the labels from the error of the surrogate and reporting intervals with finite sample coverage. The methods will be benchmarked on a public synthetic portfolio that already supplies values and sensitivities, with mortality and market dynamics calibrated to public data, and evaluated on value accuracy, sensitivity accuracy, data efficiency and interval calibration.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} variable annuities; deep learning emulation; differential machine learning; Sobolev training; pathwise sensitivities; Greeks; uncertainty quantification; conformal prediction; stochastic mortality.

\section{Introduction}
A variable annuity is a long term savings contract in which the policyholder invests in a reference fund while the insurer provides guarantees such as the guaranteed minimum accumulation, death and withdrawal benefits. These guarantees are options on the fund whose value depends jointly on the account value, interest rates, equity volatility, the policyholder's survival and the contract terms. The value has no closed form, so insurers estimate it by Monte Carlo simulation. Because a large book must be revalued repeatedly, for pricing, hedging and capital, this simulation is a major computational burden.

Neural network emulators address this burden by learning the valuation map once, from a set of simulated examples, and then evaluating it cheaply across the whole portfolio. This metamodelling approach is now well established and accurate. It has, however, a blind spot. The same Monte Carlo run that produces the value of a contract can also produce, at almost no extra cost, the sensitivities of that value to the risk factors, through pathwise differentiation or adjoint algorithmic differentiation. These sensitivities are exactly the hedging quantities an insurer needs. Standard emulators are trained on values only and throw this information away.

This proposal is built on the idea that training the emulator on values and their sensitivities together is substantially more effective than training on values alone. Matching both the function and its slopes is a strong form of regularisation: it pins down the valuation surface with far fewer training points and produces accurate sensitivities directly, rather than by differentiating a surface that was never asked to get the slopes right. This is the principle of differential machine learning and Sobolev training, and it is a natural fit for actuarial valuation, where the sensitivities are both available and wanted.

The proposal pairs this accuracy objective with a second objective of equal weight, namely uncertainty quantification. A valuation that drives a reserve or a hedge must be accompanied by an honest statement of its reliability. The emulator will therefore report, alongside each value and each sensitivity, a calibrated interval that separates the Monte Carlo noise of the simulated labels from the error of the surrogate itself. The setting throughout is stochastic mortality, so that the mortality intensity is modelled as a random process rather than a fixed life table, which is both more realistic for long dated guarantees and a point of difference from the existing emulation literature.

\section{Research Problem and Gap}
Two bodies of work meet in this proposal. The first is the emulation of variable annuity portfolios, which has shown that neural networks and related metamodels can value large books accurately and quickly, and that sensitivities can be obtained from a two level design. The second is differential machine learning, which has shown, for financial derivatives, that training on values and pathwise derivatives together gives order of magnitude improvements in accuracy and data efficiency. These two have not been brought together for insurance guarantees, and never under stochastic mortality or with calibrated uncertainty.

The gap is therefore specific. There is no variable annuity emulator that is trained jointly on values and sensitivities, that produces accurate hedging sensitivities as a direct output rather than by numerical differentiation, that reports calibrated predictive intervals on both the value and the sensitivities, and that does all of this when the mortality basis is itself stochastic. Closing this gap is the aim of the project.

\section{Research Aim, Objectives and Questions}
\subsection{Aim}
To develop and evaluate a sensitivity augmented deep learning emulator for variable annuity guarantee valuation under stochastic mortality, delivering both higher accuracy at lower simulation cost and calibrated uncertainty on values and sensitivities.

\subsection{Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item Specify the valuation problem for a stochastic mortality basis, identifying the risk factors with respect to which sensitivities are defined.
\item Build a simulation engine that returns, for each sampled contract state, a Monte Carlo value and its pathwise or adjoint sensitivities.
\item Develop the sensitivity augmented emulator and its differential training objective.
\item Quantify its accuracy and data efficiency against a value only emulator of equal capacity.
\item Attach calibrated predictive intervals to values and sensitivities, separating Monte Carlo label noise from surrogate error.
\item Evaluate the framework on a public benchmark and on data calibrated dynamics, including hedging performance.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research questions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=RQ\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item How much does training on values and sensitivities together improve value accuracy relative to training on values alone, at a fixed number of simulated contracts?
\item How many fewer simulated training points are needed to reach a target accuracy when sensitivities are included?
\item How accurate are the emulator's sensitivities, and are they good enough to hedge?
\item Can calibrated intervals with finite sample coverage be attached to both the value and the sensitivities, and how do they behave in stressed states?
\item How sensitive are accuracy and calibration to the choice and calibration of the stochastic mortality model?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Sensitivity Augmented Emulation}
\subsection{Valuation target and sensitivities}
Let $x\in\R^p$ collect the contract state and the market and mortality factors: the account value, guarantee base, age, time to maturity, fee, interest rate, equity level and volatility, and the mortality intensity and its parameters. The quantity of interest is the present value of the net guarantee liability, that is the guarantee cashflows less the rider fee income,
\begin{equation}
V(x)=\E^{\Q}\!\left[H\mid \text{state}=x\right].
\end{equation}
The same expectation defines the sensitivities, the gradient of the value with respect to the risk factors,
\begin{equation}
G(x)=\nabla_x V(x),
\end{equation}
whose components are the familiar hedging Greeks, for example the delta with respect to the equity level and the rho with respect to the interest rate, together with a mortality sensitivity.

A Monte Carlo run over $M$ inner paths returns, for a sampled state $x_i$, an unbiased value estimate and an unbiased sensitivity estimate,
\begin{equation}
\widehat V_i=\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^{M}H^{(m)}(x_i),
\qquad
\widehat G_i=\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^{M}\nabla_x H^{(m)}(x_i),
\qquad
\Var(\widehat V_i\mid x_i)=\frac{\sigma^2(x_i)}{M}.
\end{equation}
The sensitivity labels $\widehat G_i$ are obtained by pathwise differentiation or adjoint algorithmic differentiation at essentially the cost of one extra pass, so they come almost for free.

\subsection{Differential training objective}
The emulator $f_\theta:\R^p\to\R$ is trained to match both the value labels and the sensitivity labels. Writing $\nabla_x f_\theta$ for the emulator gradient obtained by automatic differentiation, the training objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(\theta)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[\big(f_\theta(x_i)-\widehat V_i\big)^2
+\sum_{k=1}^{p}\lambda_k\big(\partial_{x_k} f_\theta(x_i)-\widehat G_{i,k}\big)^2\Big],
\label{eq:sobolev}
\end{equation}
where the weights $\lambda_k$ balance the value term against each sensitivity term and may reflect the relative Monte Carlo noise of the labels. The second group of terms is what distinguishes this emulator from a standard one: it forces the learned surface to have the correct slopes, which acts as a powerful regulariser and yields accurate sensitivities as a direct output. This is the differential machine learning and Sobolev training principle, here applied to insurance guarantees.

\subsection{Why this helps}
Matching slopes supplies, at each training point, not one scalar constraint but $p+1$ constraints on the surface. The effective information per simulated contract is therefore much larger, which is expected to raise accuracy at a fixed training budget and, equivalently, to reach a target accuracy with far fewer simulated contracts. Because the hedging sensitivities are learned rather than recovered by differentiating a value only fit, they are expected to be markedly more stable, which matters directly for hedging a guarantee book.

\section{Uncertainty Quantification}
Accuracy is only half of what a reserve or a hedge requires. The emulator must also say how far each prediction can be trusted. The predictive error has two sources: the Monte Carlo noise of the labels, which is aleatoric and controlled by the inner path count, and the approximation and estimation error of the surrogate, which is epistemic. The project will estimate the epistemic part with deep ensembles and the aleatoric part from the known label variance $\sigma^2(x)/M$, and will then wrap the prediction in a split conformal layer so that the reported interval
\begin{equation}
C_\alpha(x)=\big[f_\theta(x)-q_{1-\alpha}\,s(x),\ f_\theta(x)+q_{1-\alpha}\,s(x)\big]
\end{equation}
has finite sample marginal coverage at level $1-\alpha$, where $s(x)$ is the predictive scale and $q_{1-\alpha}$ a calibration quantile. Because the training states are sampled independently from a design, the exchangeability that conformal coverage requires holds by construction. The same machinery will be applied to the sensitivities, so that the emulator reports a calibrated interval not only for the value but for each Greek. Calibration will be assessed by empirical coverage, interval width and the continuous ranked probability score.

\section{Stochastic Mortality Setting}
Mortality is modelled as a stochastic process rather than a fixed table. For a policyholder aged $x$ with future lifetime $\tau_x$ and stochastic force of mortality $\mu_{x+t}$, survival over $[0,t]$ is
\begin{equation}
{}_tp_x=\E\!\left[\exp\!\Big(-\!\int_0^t \mu_{x+s}\dd s\Big)\right],
\end{equation}
with a tractable affine intensity as the baseline specification and a longer memory alternative as a robustness check. The mortality intensity and its parameters enter the state $x$, so that mortality sensitivities are defined on the same footing as market sensitivities, and the stochastic mortality model is what generates the death timing in the simulated labels. Keeping two mortality specifications lets the study separate the emulation question from the choice of mortality model.

\section{Data}
The project uses public data throughout. No individual policyholder records are required.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Benchmark portfolio with values and sensitivities.} The Gan and Valdez synthetic variable annuity portfolio provides about $190{,}000$ contracts across nineteen guarantee types, each with a Monte Carlo fair value and Greeks. Because it already supplies values and sensitivities, it is an ideal first benchmark for the differential emulator and for comparison with the published metamodelling results.
\item \textbf{Mortality.} The Human Mortality Database supplies death rates and life tables for calibrating the stochastic mortality model. The optional South African case study uses Statistics South Africa and Actuarial Society of South Africa tables.
\item \textbf{Market.} Interest rates, equity index levels and volatility are taken from public sources such as the Federal Reserve Economic Data service and the United States Treasury, to calibrate the equity and short rate dynamics.
\item \textbf{Generated labels.} For the stochastic mortality extension, values and pathwise sensitivities are generated with an open source actuarial simulation engine, logged with seeds for reproducibility.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental Design and Evaluation}
Training states are drawn by a space filling design over the contract and market and mortality ranges, with additional density where the guarantee moves between in the money and out of the money. The central experiment compares the sensitivity augmented emulator with a value only emulator of identical architecture, as a function of the number of simulated contracts, to measure both the accuracy gain and the data efficiency gain. Value accuracy is measured by root mean squared error and mean absolute error against held out Monte Carlo values; sensitivity accuracy is measured against held out Monte Carlo Greeks and, operationally, by the profit and loss of a hedge driven by the emulator sensitivities. Uncertainty is assessed by empirical and conditional coverage, interval width and proper scores, on both values and sensitivities, including in stressed states that are sparse in the training design.

\section{Expected Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
\item A sensitivity augmented deep learning emulator for variable annuity guarantees under stochastic mortality, with a differential training objective that uses the pathwise sensitivities the simulation already produces.
\item Evidence on the accuracy and data efficiency gains from sensitivity augmentation relative to value only emulation, at matched capacity and budget.
\item Accurate, directly learned hedging sensitivities and a demonstration of their use in hedging.
\item Calibrated predictive intervals on values and sensitivities, separating Monte Carlo label noise from surrogate error.
\item An open benchmark and reproducible code for sensitivity augmented actuarial emulation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Ethics and Reproducibility}
The research uses public, aggregated mortality and market data and a public synthetic contract portfolio, so it involves no identifiable policyholder information and no human participants. Ethics clearance will be obtained from the University of the Free State before data work begins. All designs, seeds and code will be version controlled and released so that every result can be reproduced.
