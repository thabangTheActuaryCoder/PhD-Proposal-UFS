\begin{abstract}
This proposal develops a mathematical and statistical framework for fast and uncertainty aware valuation of variable annuity (VA) guarantees in the presence of stochastic mortality. Variable annuities combine investment linked accumulation with embedded guarantees such as guaranteed minimum maturity, death, income, accumulation and withdrawal benefits. Realistic valuation is computationally demanding because the liability depends jointly on financial market dynamics, mortality or longevity risk, policyholder behaviour and contract specific guarantee states. In high dimensional settings the benchmark valuation often requires Monte Carlo or nested Monte Carlo simulation, and, when policyholder decisions are endogenous, stochastic optimal control or optimal stopping methods. The proposed research uses a deep neural network as a statistical emulator of a high fidelity actuarial valuation engine while explicitly quantifying the uncertainty introduced by simulation noise, stochastic mortality, parameter estimation and neural network approximation.

The mathematical framework is built on a filtered probability space supporting the financial market and mortality processes. The account value is modelled under a risk neutral measure, with extensions from geometric Brownian motion to stochastic volatility or rough volatility dynamics. Mortality is represented by a stochastic force of mortality, with baseline affine diffusion and more advanced Volterra specifications to capture long range dependence. Survival contingent guarantees are valued as discounted expectations under an appropriate product measure, while contracts with dynamic withdrawals or surrender rights are formulated through Bellman recursion, optimal stopping or least squares Monte Carlo. The emulator learns the conditional valuation map from policy and market state variables to liability values. Uncertainty quantification is then developed using heteroscedastic likelihood networks, deep ensembles, bootstrap procedures, Monte Carlo dropout, conformalized quantile regression and explicit Monte Carlo standard error modelling.

The research will investigate pointwise and portfolio level valuation accuracy, calibration, interval coverage, proper scoring rules, tail reliability, hedge sensitivity stability and robustness under market and mortality distribution shift. The expected contribution is a statistically principled and actuarially interpretable framework that produces fast VA valuations together with defensible uncertainty intervals and model risk diagnostics.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} variable annuities; stochastic mortality; deep neural networks; uncertainty quantification; nested Monte Carlo; actuarial valuation; longevity risk; stochastic control; least squares Monte Carlo; conformal prediction.

\section{Introduction and Background}
Variable annuities are long term insurance contracts in which the policyholder participates in the performance of an investment fund while the insurer provides embedded guarantees. Common guarantees include the Guaranteed Minimum Maturity Benefit (GMMB), Guaranteed Minimum Death Benefit (GMDB), Guaranteed Minimum Accumulation Benefit (GMAB), Guaranteed Minimum Income Benefit (GMIB) and Guaranteed Minimum Withdrawal Benefit (GMWB). These benefits have option like characteristics, but their valuation differs materially from that of a standard traded derivative because insurance cash flows also depend on survival, death, withdrawals, surrender and other policyholder states.

The combination of market and biometric risks makes variable annuity valuation a natural problem in actuarial mathematics and mathematical statistics. The liability may be strongly nonlinear in the account value, guarantee base, mortality intensity, interest rate, volatility and time to maturity. It may also be path dependent. When the policyholder can surrender or withdraw optimally, the valuation problem becomes an optimal stopping or stochastic control problem rather than a simple expectation.

Classical numerical approaches include tree methods, finite differences, quadrature, dynamic programming and Monte Carlo simulation. As the dimension of the stochastic state increases, deterministic grids become increasingly costly. Recent research therefore turns to least squares Monte Carlo and neural network regression for high dimensional VA pricing. The main limitation is that most neural network metamodels return point estimates. For actuarial risk management, however, the uncertainty around the estimate is itself economically important. A reserve, hedge or capital calculation based on a machine learning surrogate should report not only the predicted liability but also how reliable that prediction is.

The central theme of this PhD is therefore
\[
\boxed{\text{Stochastic mortality} + \text{VA valuation} + \text{deep emulation} + \text{uncertainty quantification}.}
\]
The project is intended to retain substantial mathematical depth by treating the VA pricing functional, stochastic mortality model, simulation estimator and neural network emulator as linked stochastic objects rather than as a generic prediction pipeline.

\section{Problem Statement}
Let $X$ denote the full state of a VA contract, including financial variables, mortality variables and policy specific characteristics. Suppose the actuarial valuation target is
\begin{equation}
V(x)=\E^{\Q}[H\mid X_0=x],
\label{eq:valuation-generic}
\end{equation}
where $H$ is the discounted future net liability. In realistic VA settings, $H$ may depend on a high dimensional stochastic path, and $V(x)$ cannot be evaluated analytically. A Monte Carlo estimator
\begin{equation}
\widehat V_M(x)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}H^{(m)}(x)
\label{eq:mc-estimator}
\end{equation}
is therefore used as the benchmark. However, repeated evaluation of \eqref{eq:mc-estimator} across large portfolios, time steps, stresses and hedging scenarios can be prohibitively expensive.

A deep neural network $f_\theta$ can be trained to emulate the map $x\mapsto V(x)$, yielding
\begin{equation}
\widehat V_{\mathrm{NN}}(x)=f_\theta(x).
\end{equation}
The key difficulty is that the training target $\widehat V_M(x)$ is itself noisy and the fitted emulator has finite sample and approximation error. Consequently, the total uncertainty is not a single quantity. It may be decomposed schematically as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{total}}
=
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{MC}}
+
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{mort}}
+
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{param}}
+
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{NN}}
+
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{spec}},
\label{eq:uncertainty-components}
\end{equation}
where the terms correspond respectively to Monte Carlo error, mortality process uncertainty, parameter estimation uncertainty, neural network uncertainty and model specification uncertainty. The proposed PhD asks how these components can be quantified, calibrated and propagated into actuarial decision quantities.

\section{Research Aim, Objectives and Questions}
\subsection{Aim}
The main aim is to develop and evaluate a deep neural network emulation framework with statistically rigorous uncertainty quantification for variable annuity guarantee valuation under stochastic mortality.

\subsection{Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Formulate a stochastic actuarial model for VA guarantees in which financial and mortality risks evolve dynamically.
\item Construct a high fidelity Monte Carlo or least squares Monte Carlo benchmark valuation engine.
\item Develop deep neural network emulators for policy level and portfolio level liability valuation.
\item Quantify uncertainty using heteroscedastic likelihoods, ensembles, bootstrap methods, Monte Carlo dropout and conformal prediction.
\item Separate Monte Carlo noise, mortality model uncertainty, parameter uncertainty and surrogate model uncertainty where possible.
\item Evaluate calibration, coverage, sharpness, tail reliability and robustness under distribution shift.
\item Assess the propagation of emulator uncertainty into reserves, hedge sensitivities, value at risk and conditional tail expectation.
\item Establish numerical convergence results or finite sample error decompositions for the proposed estimator under suitable assumptions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research questions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=RQ\arabic*.]
\item How accurately can deep neural networks emulate VA guarantee values when mortality is stochastic?
\item How large is the computational saving relative to full Monte Carlo or nested simulation?
\item Which uncertainty quantification methods provide the best calibrated predictive distributions for VA liabilities?
\item How should total valuation uncertainty be decomposed into stochastic, parametric and approximation components?
\item How sensitive are prices and predictive intervals to the choice of stochastic mortality model?
\item Do the resulting intervals remain reliable for stressed market conditions, high moneyness and tail liability states?
\item Can emulator uncertainty be propagated reliably to actuarial capital and hedging quantities?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Actuarial and Mathematical Literature Context}
Variable annuity guaranteed benefits resemble exotic options but remain life contingent insurance liabilities. For example, a GMAB may be viewed as a put style payoff contingent on survival, while GMDB cash flows are triggered by death. This distinction means that financial and mortality risk must be handled jointly. Feng and Yi formulate stochastic reserving and hedging for VA guarantees using explicit survival contingent liabilities and actuarial tail risk measures such as VaR and CTE. More recent research extends VA models to stochastic volatility, stochastic interest rates and non Markovian mortality.

A particularly relevant development is the use of Volterra mortality models jointly with rough Heston financial dynamics. In such models the force of mortality may exhibit long range dependence and the volatility process may be non Markovian. This destroys the low dimensional Markov structure required by standard dynamic programming, motivating path based regression methods and neural approximators. Recent work also shows that the pricing error in deep signature LSMC can be decomposed into Monte Carlo sampling, time discretisation, signature truncation and neural network approximation components. This decomposition motivates the present proposal's broader statistical uncertainty analysis.

For dynamic withdrawals, the VA valuation problem is naturally formulated as stochastic control. Let $X_n$ be the state immediately before the policyholder decision at time $t_n$, and let $\pi_n$ denote the withdrawal or control. Then a controlled transition may be written as
\begin{equation}
X_{n+1}=\mathcal T_n(X_n,\pi_n,Z_{n+1}),
\end{equation}
with $Z_{n+1}$ the exogenous disturbance. The optimal value satisfies a Bellman recursion and the conditional continuation value can be estimated using least squares Monte Carlo, with the regression function replaced by a neural network in high dimensions.

\section{Mathematical Framework}
\subsection{Filtered probability space}
Let
\[
(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb F,\Pp),
\qquad
\mathbb F=(\mathcal F_t)_{0\le t\le T},
\]
be a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions. The filtration is assumed to contain the information generated by financial market factors and mortality factors. Let $\Q$ denote a risk neutral pricing measure for hedgeable financial risk. Since mortality risk is generally not fully traded, the pricing measure may be taken as a product of the financial risk neutral measure with a chosen actuarial mortality measure under independence, or more generally as a pricing functional determined by a mortality risk premium.

\subsection{Financial market model}
A baseline account model under $\Q$ is
\begin{align}
\dd S_t &= r_tS_t\dd t+\sigma_tS_t\dd W_t^{S},\label{eq:asset}\\
\dd A_t &= A_t\bigl[(r_t-\alpha)\dd t+\sigma_t\dd W_t^{S}\bigr]-\dd C_t,
\label{eq:account}
\end{align}
where $S_t$ is the reference fund, $A_t$ the VA account value, $r_t$ the short rate, $\alpha$ the total fee rate, and $C_t$ cumulative withdrawals.

For stochastic volatility one may adopt a Heston model
\begin{align}
\dd S_t &= r_tS_t\dd t + S_t\sqrt{V_t}\dd W_t^S,\label{eq:heston1}\\
\dd V_t &= \kappa_V(\theta_V-V_t)\dd t+\xi\sqrt{V_t}\dd W_t^V,\label{eq:heston2}\\
\dd\langle W^S,W^V\rangle_t&=\rho\dd t.
\end{align}
A rough Heston extension replaces the Markovian variance equation by the Volterra form
\begin{equation}
V_t
=V_0+\int_0^t K_V(t-s)
\left[\kappa_V(\theta_V-V_s)\dd s+\xi\sqrt{V_s}\dd W_s^V\right],
\label{eq:rough-heston}
\end{equation}
with fractional kernel
\begin{equation}
K_V(u)=\frac{u^{H_V-1/2}}{\Gamma(H_V+1/2)},
\qquad H_V\in(0,1/2).
\end{equation}
The fee deducted account can then be represented as
\begin{equation}
A_t=S_t\exp(-\alpha t)
\label{eq:fee-account}
\end{equation}
in the no withdrawal case.

\subsection{Stochastic interest rates}
Where stochastic rates are required, a Cox, Ingersoll and Ross specification may be used:
\begin{equation}
\dd r_t=\kappa_r(\theta_r-r_t)\dd t+\sigma_r\sqrt{r_t}\dd W_t^r,
\end{equation}
with the Feller condition $2\kappa_r\theta_r\ge\sigma_r^2$ imposed where positivity is required. The stochastic discount factor is
\begin{equation}
D(t,u)=\exp\left(-\int_t^u r_s\dd s\right).
\label{eq:discount-factor}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Mortality intensity and survival probability}
Let $\tau_x$ denote the future lifetime of a policyholder aged $x$ at issue and let $\mu_{x+t}$ denote the stochastic force of mortality. Conditional on the mortality filtration $\mathcal G_t$, survival from $t$ to $u$ is
\begin{equation}
{}_{u-t}p_{x+t}
=\Pp(\tau_x>u\mid\mathcal G_t)
=\E\left[
\exp\left(-\int_t^u\mu_{x+s}\dd s\right)
\Bigm|\mathcal G_t
\right].
\label{eq:survival}
\end{equation}
A tractable baseline diffusion is
\begin{equation}
\dd \mu_t=\kappa_\mu(\theta_\mu-\mu_t)\dd t+\sigma_\mu g(\mu_t)\dd W_t^\mu,
\label{eq:mortality-diffusion}
\end{equation}
where $g$ is selected to preserve admissibility or positivity.

A more advanced specification, motivated by recent actuarial work on long range dependence, is the Volterra mortality model
\begin{equation}
\mu_{x+t}
=\mu_x+
\int_0^t K_\mu(t-u)b(\mu_{x+u})\dd u
+
\int_0^t K_\mu(t-u)\sigma(\mu_{x+u})\dd B_u,
\label{eq:volterra-mortality-general}
\end{equation}
with fractional kernel
\begin{equation}
K_\mu(u)=\frac{u^{H_\mu-1/2}}{\Gamma(H_\mu+1/2)}.
\label{eq:mortality-kernel}
\end{equation}
A one factor affine specification is
\begin{equation}
\mu_{x+t}
=
\mu_x
+
\lambda_\mu\int_0^tK_\mu(t-u)\mu_{x+u}\dd u
+
\sigma_\mu\int_0^tK_\mu(t-u)\sqrt{\mu_{x+u}}\dd B_u.
\label{eq:volterra-mortality}
\end{equation}
When $H_\mu>1/2$, the kernel can induce long range dependence, while $H_\mu=1/2$ recovers a Markovian limit.

For discrete numerical paths on a grid $0=t_0<\cdots<t_N$, survival along a simulated mortality path can be approximated by the trapezoidal rule,
\begin{equation}
{}_{t_n}p_x^{(m)}
\approx
\exp\left
\{-\frac{\Delta t}{2}
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\left(\mu_{x+t_k}^{(m)}+\mu_{x+t_{k+1}}^{(m)}\right)
\right\}.
\label{eq:survival-discrete}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Mortality calibration}
If observed cohort survival proportions are $\ell_{x+s}/\ell_x$, mortality parameters $\vartheta_\mu$ may be estimated by nonlinear least squares,
\begin{equation}
\widehat\vartheta_\mu
=
\argmin_{\vartheta_\mu\in\Theta_\mu}
\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N}
\left[
{}_sp_x(\vartheta_\mu)-\frac{\ell_{x+s}}{\ell_x}
\right]^2.
\label{eq:mortality-calibration}
\end{equation}
Parameter uncertainty may then be studied by nonparametric bootstrap, parametric bootstrap or Bayesian posterior sampling.

\subsection{Guaranteed benefits}
For a maturity guarantee $G_T$, the insurer's gross GMMB liability is
\begin{equation}
L_T^{\mathrm{GMMB}}
=
\ind_{\{\tau_x>T\}}
D(0,T)(G_T-A_T)^+.
\label{eq:gmmb}
\end{equation}
For a death benefit $G_t^D$, the GMDB liability can be represented as
\begin{equation}
L^{\mathrm{GMDB}}
=
\ind_{\{\tau_x\le T\}}
D(0,\tau_x)(G_{\tau_x}^D-A_{\tau_x})^+.
\label{eq:gmdb}
\end{equation}
A combined guarantee may therefore be written
\begin{equation}
H
=
\ind_{\{\tau_x>T\}}D(0,T)(G_T-A_T)^+
+
\ind_{\{\tau_x\le T\}}D(0,\tau_x)(G_{\tau_x}^D-A_{\tau_x})^+.
\label{eq:combined-payoff}
\end{equation}

If fees at rate $\alpha_g$ are collected to fund the guarantee, the discounted fee income is
\begin{equation}
I
=
\alpha_g\int_0^{T\wedge\tau_x}D(0,t)A_t\dd t,
\end{equation}
and the net liability is
\begin{equation}
L=H-I.
\label{eq:net-liability}
\end{equation}
The distinction between gross and net liability is actuarially important because adverse markets may simultaneously increase guarantee costs and reduce fee income.

\subsection{Market consistent valuation}
The fair value of the guarantee is
\begin{equation}
V_0=\E^{\Q}[L\mid\mathcal F_0].
\label{eq:fair-value}
\end{equation}
For a fee parameter $c$, the fair fee $c^*$ is defined implicitly by
\begin{equation}
U_0(c^*)=A_0,
\label{eq:fair-fee}
\end{equation}
where $U_0(c)$ is the value of the full contract under fee level $c$. If $U_0(c)$ is monotone decreasing, $c^*$ can be obtained by bisection.

\section{Optimal Stopping and Stochastic Control Extension}
Many VA contracts allow surrender or dynamic withdrawals. Let the decision grid be
\[
0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_N=T,
\]
and let $X_n$ denote the full state vector before the decision at $t_n$. For a withdrawal $\pi_n\in\mathcal A_n(X_n)$,
\begin{equation}
X_{n+1}=\mathcal T_n(X_n,\pi_n,Z_{n+1}).
\label{eq:controlled-transition}
\end{equation}
Let $R_n(X_n,\pi_n)$ denote the immediate policyholder cash flow and $\beta_{n,n+1}=D(t_n,t_{n+1})$. The optimal value function satisfies
\begin{equation}
V_n(x)=
\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal A_n(x)}
\left\{
R_n(x,\pi)
+
\E^{\Q}\left[
\beta_{n,n+1}V_{n+1}(X_{n+1})
\mid X_n=x,\pi_n=\pi
\right]
\right\},
\label{eq:bellman}
\end{equation}
with terminal condition
\begin{equation}
V_N(x)=R_N(x).
\end{equation}
Define the continuation function
\begin{equation}
\Phi_n(x,\pi)
=
\E^{\Q}\left[
\beta_{n,n+1}V_{n+1}(X_{n+1})
\mid X_n=x,\pi_n=\pi
\right].
\label{eq:continuation}
\end{equation}
Least squares Monte Carlo approximates \eqref{eq:continuation} by regression,
\begin{equation}
\beta_{n,n+1}V_{n+1}(X_{n+1}^{(m)})
=f_{\theta_n}(X_n^{(m)},\pi_n^{(m)})+\varepsilon_n^{(m)}.
\label{eq:lsmc-regression}
\end{equation}
A neural network is attractive because $f_{\theta_n}$ may be highly nonlinear and high dimensional.

For pure surrender, with immediate surrender payoff $g_n(X_n)$, the recursion simplifies to
\begin{equation}
V_n(X_n)=\max\left\{g_n(X_n),\Phi_n(X_n)\right\}.
\label{eq:optimal-stopping}
\end{equation}
The optimal stopping time is
\begin{equation}
\tau^*=\inf\{t_n: g_n(X_n)\ge \Phi_n(X_n)\}.
\label{eq:stopping-time}
\end{equation}

\section{Monte Carlo Valuation and Statistical Error}
\subsection{Monte Carlo estimator}
For each policy state $x$, suppose the discounted liability is $H(x,\omega)$. The benchmark estimator is
\begin{equation}
\widehat V_M(x)=\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^{M}H(x,\omega_m).
\end{equation}
Conditional on $X=x$,
\begin{equation}
\E[\widehat V_M(x)\mid x]=V(x),
\qquad
\Var(\widehat V_M(x)\mid x)
=\frac{\sigma_H^2(x)}{M},
\label{eq:mc-var}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\sigma_H^2(x)=\Var(H(x,\omega)\mid X=x).
\end{equation}
The empirical Monte Carlo standard error is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\operatorname{SE}}_{\mathrm{MC}}(x)
=
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{M(M-1)}
\sum_{m=1}^{M}
\left(H^{(m)}(x)-\widehat V_M(x)\right)^2
}.
\label{eq:mc-se}
\end{equation}
Thus the supervised response is naturally heteroscedastic across policy states.

Under standard finite variance conditions,
\begin{equation}
\sqrt{M}\left(\widehat V_M(x)-V(x)\right)
\xrightarrow{d}
\mathcal N(0,\sigma_H^2(x)).
\label{eq:mc-clt}
\end{equation}
This provides a first layer of asymptotic uncertainty quantification.

\subsection{Nested simulation}
For nested simulation, let $Z_i$ be an outer economic state and $Y_{ij}$ the inner liability draw. Then
\begin{equation}
\widehat V_{M,N}
=
\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left(
\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^{M}Y_{ij}
\right).
\end{equation}
The total simulation error depends on both outer and inner sample sizes. The project will study optimal allocation under a fixed computational budget $B\approx NM$ and how emulation changes this budget trade off.

\subsection{Variance reduction}
Antithetic variates, control variates, common random numbers and quasi Monte Carlo will be considered. If $C$ is a control with known expectation, an adjusted estimator is
\begin{equation}
\widehat V_{\mathrm{CV}}
=
\widehat V_M
-b\left(\overline C_M-\E[C]\right),
\end{equation}
with optimal coefficient
\begin{equation}
b^*=\frac{\Cov(H,C)}{\Var(C)}.
\end{equation}

\section{Deep Neural Network Emulator}
Let $x\in\R^p$ denote the transformed policy and market state. A feedforward network with $L$ hidden layers is
\begin{align}
h^{(0)}&=x,\\
h^{(\ell)}&=\phi_\ell\left(W^{(\ell)}h^{(\ell-1)}+b^{(\ell)}\right),
\qquad \ell=1,\ldots,L,\\
f_\theta(x)&=W^{(L+1)}h^{(L)}+b^{(L+1)}.
\end{align}
The standard empirical risk estimator is
\begin{equation}
\widehat\theta
=
\argmin_{\theta}
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}
w_i\left(\widehat V_{M_i}(x_i)-f_\theta(x_i)\right)^2
+\lambda\mathcal R(\theta),
\label{eq:nn-loss}
\end{equation}
where $w_i$ may give additional weight to tail liabilities, and $\mathcal R$ is a regularisation penalty.

If Monte Carlo standard errors are available, a statistically efficient weighting choice is
\begin{equation}
w_i\propto \widehat{\operatorname{SE}}_{\mathrm{MC}}(x_i)^{-2},
\end{equation}
provided excessively small estimated standard errors are stabilised.

\subsection{Approximation and estimation error}
Let $f^*(x)=V(x)$. A useful conceptual decomposition is
\begin{align}
\widehat f(x)-f^*(x)
&=
\underbrace{\left(\widehat f(x)-f_{\mathcal F}^*(x)\right)}_{\text{estimation or optimisation}}
+
\underbrace{\left(f_{\mathcal F}^*(x)-f^*(x)\right)}_{\text{approximation}},
\label{eq:nn-error-decomp}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathcal F}^*=\argmin_{f\in\mathcal F}\E[(V(X)-f(X))^2].
\end{equation}
The observed response additionally contains Monte Carlo noise,
\begin{equation}
\widehat V_M(x)=V(x)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{MC}}(x),
\qquad
\E[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{MC}}(x)\mid x]=0.
\end{equation}

\section{Uncertainty Quantification}
\subsection{Aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty}
For predictive parameter $\Theta$ and training data $\mathcal D$, the law of total variance gives
\begin{equation}
\Var(Y^*\mid x,\mathcal D)
=
\E_{\Theta\mid\mathcal D}
\left[
\Var(Y^*\mid x,\Theta)
\right]
+
\Var_{\Theta\mid\mathcal D}
\left[
\E(Y^*\mid x,\Theta)
\right].
\label{eq:total-variance}
\end{equation}
The first term is interpreted as conditional or aleatoric uncertainty and the second as epistemic model uncertainty. In the present problem, the first term will be refined to separate intrinsic path variation from finite Monte Carlo target noise.

\subsection{Heteroscedastic probabilistic network}
A Gaussian working model is
\begin{equation}
Y_i\mid X_i=x_i
\sim
\mathcal N\left(\mu_\theta(x_i),\sigma_\theta^2(x_i)\right).
\end{equation}
The negative log likelihood is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}(\theta)
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left[
\frac{(y_i-\mu_\theta(x_i))^2}{2\sigma_\theta^2(x_i)}
+\frac12\log\sigma_\theta^2(x_i)
\right].
\label{eq:hetero-nll}
\end{equation}
To ensure positivity, the scale output can be parameterised as
\begin{equation}
\sigma_\theta(x)=\log(1+\exp(s_\theta(x)))+\epsilon.
\end{equation}
Student $t$ likelihoods will be considered if residuals are heavy tailed.

\subsection{Deep ensembles}
Train $K$ independently initialised networks. If network $k$ produces mean $\mu_k(x)$ and variance $\sigma_k^2(x)$, then
\begin{equation}
\overline\mu(x)=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mu_k(x),
\end{equation}
and the total predictive variance estimate is
\begin{equation}
\widehat\sigma_{\mathrm{ens}}^2(x)
=
\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sigma_k^2(x)
+
\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mu_k^2(x)
-
\overline\mu^2(x).
\label{eq:ensemble-var}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Monte Carlo dropout}
With dropout retained at inference, perform $B$ stochastic forward passes $f_{\theta,b}(x)$. Then
\begin{equation}
\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{MCdrop}}(x)
=
\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^{B}f_{\theta,b}(x),
\end{equation}
with predictive variance estimated from the empirical dispersion of the forward passes, optionally augmented by observation noise variance.

\subsection{Bootstrap neural networks}
For bootstrap datasets $\mathcal D^{*(1)},\ldots,\mathcal D^{*(B)}$, fit $f_{\widehat\theta^{*(b)}}$. A percentile interval for $V(x)$ is
\begin{equation}
\left[
q_{\alpha/2}\{f_{\widehat\theta^{*(b)}}(x)\},
q_{1-\alpha/2}\{f_{\widehat\theta^{*(b)}}(x)\}
\right].
\end{equation}
A two level bootstrap will additionally resample mortality parameters or mortality data before regenerating valuation targets.

\subsection{Conformal prediction}
For split conformal regression, fit $\widehat f$ on a training sample and define calibration residuals
\begin{equation}
r_i=|y_i-\widehat f(x_i)|,
\qquad i\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{cal}}.
\end{equation}
Let $\widehat q_{1-\alpha}$ denote the appropriate empirical quantile. Then
\begin{equation}
C_{1-\alpha}(x)
=
\left[
\widehat f(x)-\widehat q_{1-\alpha},
\widehat f(x)+\widehat q_{1-\alpha}
\right]
\label{eq:conformal-simple}
\end{equation}
has finite sample marginal coverage under exchangeability.

For conformalized quantile regression, first estimate lower and upper conditional quantiles
\begin{equation}
\widehat q_{\alpha/2}(x),
\qquad
\widehat q_{1-\alpha/2}(x),
\end{equation}
and calibrate with scores
\begin{equation}
R_i
=
\max\left\{
\widehat q_{\alpha/2}(x_i)-y_i,
\ y_i-\widehat q_{1-\alpha/2}(x_i)
\right\}.
\end{equation}
The calibrated interval becomes
\begin{equation}
\left[
\widehat q_{\alpha/2}(x)-\widehat Q,
\widehat q_{1-\alpha/2}(x)+\widehat Q
\right].
\label{eq:cqr}
\end{equation}

\section{Error Decomposition and Convergence Targets}
A central theoretical objective will be to derive an error decomposition of the form
\begin{equation}
|\widehat V_{\mathrm{final}}(x)-V(x)|
\le
E_{\mathrm{MC}}(x)
+E_{\mathrm{time}}(x)
+E_{\mathrm{mort}}(x)
+E_{\mathrm{approx}}(x)
+E_{\mathrm{est}}(x)
+E_{\mathrm{opt}}(x),
\label{eq:master-error}
\end{equation}
where the components respectively denote Monte Carlo sampling error, time discretisation, mortality discretisation or calibration error, neural network approximation error, statistical estimation error and numerical optimisation error.

For a path dependent extension based on truncated signatures, an additional term enters,
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{sig}}(K),
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the truncation level. The target result would establish conditions under which
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{MC}}
+E_{\mathrm{time}}
+E_{\mathrm{sig}}
+E_{\mathrm{approx}}
+E_{\mathrm{est}}
\longrightarrow 0
\end{equation}
as simulation size, time resolution, signature depth and network capacity increase in a controlled sequence.

A possible asymptotic statement for the emulator is consistency in $L^2(P_X)$,
\begin{equation}
\int
\left|
\widehat f_n(x)-V(x)
\right|^2\dd P_X(x)
\xrightarrow{P}0,
\label{eq:l2-consistency}
\end{equation}
under assumptions on compactness or tail control of $X$, universal approximation of the network class, vanishing optimisation error and increasing effective simulation accuracy.

\section{Actuarial Risk Measures and Capital}
Let $L$ denote the one year or runoff net liability distribution. For confidence level $\alpha\in(0,1)$,
\begin{equation}
\VaR_\alpha(L)
=
\inf\{\ell\in\R:\Pp(L\le \ell)\ge \alpha\}.
\label{eq:var}
\end{equation}
For continuous distributions,
\begin{equation}
\CTE_\alpha(L)
=
\E[L\mid L>\VaR_\alpha(L)].
\label{eq:cte}
\end{equation}
The effect of emulator uncertainty on these quantities will be studied by comparing
\begin{equation}
\VaR_\alpha(L)
\quad\text{versus}\quad
\VaR_\alpha(\widehat L_{\mathrm{NN}}),
\end{equation}
and similarly for CTE. The error
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{VaR}}
=
\VaR_\alpha(\widehat L_{\mathrm{NN}})
-
\VaR_\alpha(L)
\end{equation}
will be analysed across simulation replicates and model stresses.

\section{Hedging Sensitivities}
For a differentiable emulator, automatic differentiation permits estimates of valuation sensitivities. If $S$ is the underlying equity state,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{NN}}
=
\frac{\partial f_\theta(x)}{\partial S},
\qquad
\Gamma_{\mathrm{NN}}
=
\frac{\partial^2 f_\theta(x)}{\partial S^2}.
\end{equation}
Similarly,
\begin{equation}
\nu_{\mathrm{NN}}
=
\frac{\partial f_\theta(x)}{\partial \sigma},
\qquad
\rho_{\mathrm{NN}}
=
\frac{\partial f_\theta(x)}{\partial r}.
\end{equation}
A mortality sensitivity can be defined by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M_{\mathrm{NN}}
=
\frac{\partial f_\theta(x)}{\partial \mu},
\end{equation}
or with respect to mortality model parameters. These derivatives will be compared with finite difference Monte Carlo estimators using common random numbers.

\section{Experimental Design}
Let the state vector be
\begin{equation}
X=
\left(
A_0,
G_0,
x,
T,
\alpha,
r_0,
V_0,
\mu_x,
\vartheta_{\mu},
\vartheta_{\mathrm{mkt}}
\right).
\end{equation}
Training design points will be sampled using Latin hypercube or space filling designs. The design density will be augmented near important regions such as
\begin{equation}
\frac{G_0}{A_0}\approx 1,
\end{equation}
where the guarantee moves between in the money and out of the money states, and in stressed regions characterised by low account values, high volatility or adverse mortality assumptions.

The simulation dataset will contain
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D
=
\left\{
\left(x_i,\widehat V_{M_i}(x_i),\widehat{\operatorname{SE}}_{\mathrm{MC}}(x_i)\right)
\right\}_{i=1}^{n}.
\end{equation}
Replicated valuations at a subset of states will permit direct validation of the estimated target noise model.

\section{Model Evaluation}
\subsection{Point prediction metrics}
\begin{align}
\mathrm{MAE}
&=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_i-\widehat y_i|,\\
\mathrm{RMSE}
&=
\sqrt{\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\widehat y_i)^2},\\
\mathrm{MAPE}
&=
\frac{100}{n}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left|\frac{y_i-\widehat y_i}{y_i}\right|,
\end{align}
with MAPE used only when denominators are suitably bounded away from zero.

\subsection{Interval calibration}
For predictive intervals $[L_i,U_i]$, empirical coverage is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{1-\alpha}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\ind\{L_i\le y_i\le U_i\}.
\end{equation}
Sharpness is summarised by
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Width}
=
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}(U_i-L_i).
\end{equation}
Conditional coverage will be evaluated across strata of age, guarantee moneyness, volatility, mortality intensity and liability quantile.

\subsection{Proper scoring rules}
For Gaussian predictive distribution $F_i=\mathcal N(\mu_i,\sigma_i^2)$, the negative log score is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NLL}_i
=
\frac12\log(2\pi\sigma_i^2)
+
\frac{(y_i-\mu_i)^2}{2\sigma_i^2}.
\end{equation}
The continuous ranked probability score is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CRPS}(F,y)
=
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\left(F(z)-\ind\{y\le z\}\right)^2\dd z.
\end{equation}
These scores jointly reward calibration and sharpness.

\section{Distribution Shift and Stress Testing}
The emulator will be tested under states not represented centrally in training. Let $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{stress}}$ denote the training and stress distributions. Performance degradation is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{shift}}
=
\mathcal R_{P_{\mathrm{stress}}}(\widehat f)
-
\mathcal R_{P_{\mathrm{train}}}(\widehat f).
\end{equation}
Stress dimensions will include equity drawdowns, volatility shocks, interest rate shifts, longevity improvements, higher mortality volatility and extreme guarantee moneyness.

A practical uncertainty based fallback rule can be defined as
\begin{equation}
\text{use full simulation if}
\quad
\widehat\sigma_{\mathrm{pred}}(x)>c_{\mathrm{UQ}}
\quad\text{or}\quad
d(x,\mathcal X_{\mathrm{train}})>c_d,
\end{equation}
where $d$ is an out of distribution distance measure. This leads to a hybrid actuarial architecture in which the emulator prices ordinary states and the original stochastic engine is retained for uncertain states.

\section{Methodology and Work Packages}
\subsection{Work Package 1: benchmark stochastic valuation}
The first stage will specify the VA rider, financial dynamics and mortality dynamics. The baseline contract will be a GMMB and GMDB combination because the mathematics of survival contingent benefits is transparent while still retaining both financial and biometric risk. An extension to GMWB or surrender will introduce stochastic control.

The valuation engine will be validated against special cases for which analytic or semi analytic benchmarks are available. Monte Carlo convergence will be assessed with increasing path counts and variance reduction methods.

\subsection{Work Package 2: mortality calibration and uncertainty}
At least two mortality specifications will be studied: a Markovian intensity model and a non Markovian Volterra model. Parameter estimates $\widehat\vartheta_\mu$ will be obtained from public mortality data. Bootstrap samples
\begin{equation}
\widehat\vartheta_\mu^{*(1)},\ldots,\widehat\vartheta_\mu^{*(B)}
\end{equation}
will generate a distribution of valuations
\begin{equation}
\widehat V^{*(b)}(x)
=
V(x;\widehat\vartheta_\mu^{*(b)}),
\end{equation}
which quantifies mortality parameter uncertainty.

\subsection{Work Package 3: neural emulator}
The emulator will be trained on simulated policy states. Candidate architectures include multilayer perceptrons with ReLU, GELU, CELU or $\tanh$ activations, residual connections, and monotonicity or positivity constraints where actuarially justified.

\subsection{Work Package 4: uncertainty quantification}
The methods in the previous section will be compared under common train and test designs. The primary target is not merely nominal coverage but conditional reliability in actuarially important regions.

\subsection{Work Package 5: optimal control extension}
If the core GMMB and GMDB framework is completed successfully, the project will extend to surrender or GMWB. Neural network LSMC will be used to approximate continuation values, and uncertainty in the estimated exercise or withdrawal boundary will be studied.

\section{Expected Mathematical Contributions}
The proposal is intended to yield more than an applied comparison of machine learning methods. Candidate mathematical and statistical contributions include:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item A formal stochastic representation of VA net liability under joint financial and stochastic mortality risk.
\item A decomposition of total valuation error into simulation, mortality, numerical discretisation, approximation and estimation components.
\item Consistency or convergence conditions for the emulator trained on noisy Monte Carlo targets.
\item Calibration results for conformal or probabilistic prediction intervals under heteroscedastic actuarial simulation noise.
\item A theoretically justified allocation of computational budget between design points and inner Monte Carlo paths.
\item Stability results for emulator derived hedge sensitivities.
\item A path dependent extension in which neural regression is combined with signatures or another functional representation for non Markovian mortality and volatility.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Expected Actuarial Contributions}
The actuarial contributions are expected to include:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item faster policy level and portfolio level valuation;
\item explicit uncertainty intervals around reserve or liability estimates;
\item quantified mortality model risk;
\item improved diagnostics for tail liabilities;
\item uncertainty aware VaR and CTE calculations;
\item more stable and computationally efficient hedge sensitivities;
\item a practical rule for when a surrogate model should defer to full stochastic simulation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Validation and Robustness}
Validation will proceed at four levels. First, the benchmark stochastic engine will be checked numerically. Second, point predictions will be compared with unseen high fidelity Monte Carlo values. Third, predictive intervals will be evaluated for empirical and conditional coverage. Fourth, all methods will be stress tested under model and distribution shift.

Where possible, the study will use paired random numbers so that differences between valuation methods have lower Monte Carlo variance. Bootstrap confidence intervals for comparative metrics will be reported. Particular attention will be paid to tail strata because overall RMSE can conceal poor performance where insurer losses are largest.

\section{Ethical Considerations and Reproducibility}
The project can be completed using synthetic policy portfolios and public mortality data, avoiding identifiable policyholder information. If proprietary data are later introduced, the study will follow institutional ethics and data governance requirements.

All simulation designs, parameter configurations and random number seeds will be version controlled. Subject to licensing constraints, code and synthetic benchmark data will be released to support reproducibility.

\section{Conclusion}
This PhD will investigate a mathematically rich actuarial problem in which financial uncertainty, mortality risk, simulation error and neural network approximation interact. The key contribution is not merely to replace Monte Carlo valuation with a neural network, but to develop an emulator whose uncertainty is measurable, calibrated and usable in actuarial risk management. The proposed mathematical framework incorporates stochastic mortality intensities, market consistent pricing, survival contingent benefits, Monte Carlo limit theory, stochastic control, nonlinear function approximation, predictive uncertainty and actuarial tail measures. This places the research firmly within mathematical statistics and actuarial mathematics while retaining strong computational relevance.
