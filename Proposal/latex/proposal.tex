	\section*{Glossary}
	\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Glossary}

	\begin{description}
		\item[Aleatoric uncertainty] The irreducible component of predictive error, here the Monte Carlo noise of the simulated labels and the genuine financial and mortality randomness.
		\item[Emulator (surrogate)] A fast approximating function, learned from expensively computed examples, that replaces a costly valuation routine.
		\item[Epistemic uncertainty] The reducible component of predictive error attributable to the emulator itself: its approximation and estimation error.
		\item[Fair fee] The rider fee rate that sets the time-zero value of the full contract equal to the premium invested.
		\item[Marginal coverage] A coverage probability averaged over calibration samples, as distinct from coverage conditional on the realised calibration sample.
		\item[Nested Monte Carlo] Valuation by an outer layer of scenarios, each carrying an inner layer of risk-neutral paths; the industry reference for guarantee valuation.
		\item[Net liability] The present value of guarantee benefits less the present value of the rider fee income that funds them.
		\item[Volterra process] A stochastic process driven through a fractional kernel, able to exhibit roughness or long-range dependence and generally non-Markovian.
	\end{description}

	\section{Introduction} % Approx. 1.5 pages

	A variable annuity (VA) is a long-term insurance contract in which the
	policyholder participates in the performance of an investment fund while the
	insurer provides one or more embedded guarantees. Common guarantees include the
	guaranteed minimum maturity, death, accumulation, income and withdrawal benefits
	(GMMB, GMDB, GMAB, GMIB, GMWB). These benefits have option-like features, but
	their valuation differs materially from that of a traded derivative because the
	insurance cashflows also depend on survival, death, withdrawals, surrender and
	other policyholder states \citep{BauerKlingRuss2008, FengYi2019}. The liability
	is therefore exposed jointly to equity, interest-rate and mortality risk, it is
	frequently high-dimensional and path-dependent, and once realistic dynamics are
	assumed it has no closed form.

	The industry reference for valuation is Monte Carlo, and often nested Monte
	Carlo, simulation: an outer layer generates economic or capital scenarios and an
	inner layer estimates the present value of future guarantee cashflows conditional
	on each outer scenario \citep{GanValdez2018}. The cost is the product of the two
	layers and grows sharply with the dimension of the state, so that revaluing a
	book of many contracts daily, under many stresses, for pricing, hedging and
	capital, is computationally demanding. When the policyholder can surrender or
	withdraw optimally, the valuation becomes an optimal stopping or stochastic
	control problem \citep{LuoShevchenko2015}, which is more expensive still.

	Emulation, or metamodelling, is the established response: the valuation function
	is learned once, from a designed set of expensively computed examples, and then
	evaluated cheaply wherever it is needed \citep{HejaziJackson2016, GanLin2015,
	GanValdez2018}. Neural networks are a natural choice because they approximate
	high-dimensional nonlinear maps without hand-crafted basis functions and value a
	whole portfolio in a single forward pass. The limitation of this literature is
	that it returns point estimates. For actuarial risk management the uncertainty
	around the estimate is itself economically important: a reserve, a hedge or a
	capital figure based on a surrogate should report not only the predicted
	liability but also how far it can be trusted.

	The central theme of this PhD is therefore the combination
	\begin{equation}
		\text{stochastic mortality} + \text{VA valuation} + \text{deep emulation}
		+ \text{uncertainty quantification}.
	\end{equation}
	The project treats the VA pricing functional, the stochastic mortality model, the
	simulation estimator and the neural-network emulator as linked stochastic objects
	rather than as a generic prediction pipeline, and places uncertainty
	quantification at the centre. It also builds on the candidate's MSc, which
	developed deep-learning methods for hedging path-dependent claims in incomplete
	markets, and redirects that craft toward emulation and uncertainty quantification
	for insurance guarantees carrying mortality risk.

	\section{Background} % Approx. 2.5 pages

	\textbf{Variable-annuity guarantees and nested valuation.}
	\citet{BauerKlingRuss2008} value the various guaranteed minimum benefits within a
	single nested-simulation architecture. A typical contract invests the premium in
	a fund, deducts a continuous fee, and promises a guarantee level $G$; the maturity
	benefit is $\max(G,A_T)$ on survival, the death benefit $\max(G,A_\tau)$ on death
	at $\tau<T$, and surrender returns the account net of a charge. It is the embedded
	shortfall options, not the account itself, that the insurer reserves against.

	\textbf{Risk management and reserving of guarantees.} \citet{FengYi2019} formalise
	the insurer's net liability as the present value of guarantee cashflows less the
	rider fee income collected from the account, and compare the two standard
	risk-management approaches: stochastic reserving based on risk measures such as
	value-at-risk (VaR) and the conditional tail expectation (CTE), and dynamic
	hedging. Their net-liability formulation and their VaR and CTE measures define,
	respectively, the valuation target of this project and the capital figures used to
	judge the practical value of its uncertainty quantification; in tractable cases
	they also obtain closed-form risk measures that serve as a known-answer benchmark.

	\textbf{Emulation and metamodelling of VA portfolios.} \citet{HejaziJackson2016}
	introduced a neural-network approach to the efficient valuation of large VA
	portfolios, and \citet{GanLin2015, GanLin2018} developed a complementary
	metamodelling programme, including functional-data and two-level designs for both
	values and Greeks. \citet{GanValdez2018} provide a synthetic-data framework for
	nested stochastic valuation of large portfolios. This literature establishes the
	feasibility and accuracy of emulation but returns point estimates under static
	mortality.

	\textbf{Deep learning solvers for VA valuation.} \citet{LiLyu2026} price a VA with
	an early-surrender option under a rough-Heston equity model and a Volterra
	stochastic-mortality model using a deep signature least-squares Monte Carlo scheme,
	and decompose the pricing error into Monte Carlo, time-discretisation,
	signature-truncation and neural-network-approximation parts. \citet{LangreneEtAl2026}
	extend deep LSMC to the GMWB under optimal dynamic withdrawal and construct
	confidence intervals for the contract value. Both are per-contract solvers that
	quantify only the Monte Carlo estimator error of a single price.

	\textbf{Stochastic mortality.} The Lee-Carter model \citep{LeeCarter1992} remains
	the reference for mortality forecasting, affine stochastic-intensity models
	\citep{Biffis2005} provide tractable continuous-time dynamics, and the
	Cairns-Blake-Dowd model \citep{CairnsBlakeDowd2006} explicitly incorporates
	parameter uncertainty. \citet{BallottaHaberman2006} value guaranteed annuity
	options under a stochastic mortality environment, establishing a precedent for
	integrated financial and mortality valuation, and \citet{Wang2021} develop a
	Volterra mortality model with long-range dependence. These establish that
	mortality risk is not limited to the randomness of future deaths: uncertainty
	about the mortality process and its parameters is itself material.

	\textbf{Stochastic control and least-squares Monte Carlo.} For dynamic withdrawals
	and surrender the valuation is a stochastic control problem. The least-squares
	Monte Carlo method \citep{LongstaffSchwartz2001} approximates continuation values
	by regression, and \citet{LuoShevchenko2015} apply stochastic control to GMWB and
	GMDB. Replacing the regression basis by a neural network extends the method to high
	dimensions.

	\textbf{Uncertainty quantification.} Deep ensembles \citep{Lakshminarayanan2017},
	Monte-Carlo dropout as approximate Bayesian inference \citep{GalGhahramani2016}, and
	the aleatoric/epistemic decomposition \citep{KendallGal2017} supply an uncertainty
	representation. Conformal prediction \citep{Vovk2005, Lei2018} and conformalized
	quantile regression \citep{RomanoEtAl2019} add distribution-free, finite-sample
	coverage, and proper scoring rules \citep{GneitingRaftery2007} assess calibration
	and sharpness together. The synthesis pursued here, a neural VA emulator whose
	predictive intervals are uncertainty-decomposed and conformally certified under
	stochastic mortality, sits at the intersection of these strands.

	\section{Research Gaps} % Approx. 0.5 page

	The recent deep-LSMC methods are per-contract solvers that quantify only Monte
	Carlo estimator error; the cross-sectional emulators return point values under
	static mortality and no uncertainty. No existing method delivers a VA-guarantee
	emulator that (i) ingests a stochastic-mortality state as a first-class input,
	(ii) returns a distribution-free, finite-sample coverage-guaranteed predictive
	interval for the guarantee value, and (iii) decomposes that interval into the
	Monte Carlo, mortality, parameter, approximation and estimation components. This
	work uses the LSMC solvers and the calibrated Volterra mortality dynamics of
	\citet{LiLyu2026} and \citet{Wang2021} as the nested-simulation ground truth and
	benchmark, not as competitors.

	\section{Research Problem and Statement} % Approx. 1 page

	Let $X$ denote the full state of a VA contract, and suppose the actuarial valuation
	target is
	\begin{equation}
		V(x) = \E^{\Q}\!\left[H \mid X_0 = x\right],
		\label{eq:valuation-generic}
	\end{equation}
	where $H$ is the discounted future net liability. In realistic settings $H$ depends
	on a high-dimensional stochastic path and $V(x)$ has no analytic form, so a Monte
	Carlo estimator
	\begin{equation}
		\widehat V_M(x) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M} H^{(m)}(x)
		\label{eq:mc-estimator}
	\end{equation}
	is used as the benchmark. A deep neural network $f_\theta$ emulates
	$x\mapsto V(x)$, giving $\widehat V_{\mathrm{NN}}(x) = f_\theta(x)$. The difficulty
	is that the training target \eqref{eq:mc-estimator} is itself noisy and the fitted
	emulator has finite-sample and approximation error, so the total uncertainty is not
	a single quantity. It decomposes schematically as
	\begin{equation}
		\mathcal U_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathcal U_{\mathrm{MC}} + \mathcal U_{\mathrm{mort}}
		+ \mathcal U_{\mathrm{param}} + \mathcal U_{\mathrm{NN}} + \mathcal U_{\mathrm{spec}},
		\label{eq:uncertainty-components}
	\end{equation}
	with terms for Monte Carlo error, mortality-process uncertainty,
	parameter-estimation uncertainty, neural-network uncertainty and
	model-specification uncertainty. The problem is to quantify, calibrate and propagate
	these components into actuarial decision quantities, so that the emulator reports an
	interval with a certified probability of containing the true value.

	\section{Thesis Statement (PhD only)} % Approx. 1-2 sentences

	A deep neural network can emulate the present value of a VA guarantee liability
	under stochastic mortality to an accuracy comparable with the Monte Carlo error of
	its training labels, and its predictions can be equipped with distribution-free,
	finite-sample coverage-guaranteed intervals whose Monte Carlo, mortality, parameter
	and surrogate components are separately identified and propagated to reserves,
	capital and hedge sensitivities.

	\section{Research Aim} % Approx. 0.5 page

	To develop and evaluate a deep neural-network emulation framework with
	statistically rigorous uncertainty quantification for variable-annuity guarantee
	valuation under stochastic mortality, and to establish the error decomposition and
	convergence guarantees that make its predictive intervals trustworthy for pricing,
	hedging and capital.

	\section{Research Questions and Objectives} % Approx. 1.5 pages

	\textbf{RQ1.} How accurately can a deep neural network emulate VA guarantee values
	when mortality is stochastic rather than deterministic?

	\textbf{RQ2.} How large is the computational saving relative to full Monte Carlo or
	nested simulation, at a given actuarial accuracy?

	\textbf{RQ3.} Which uncertainty-quantification methods give the best-calibrated and
	sharpest predictive distributions for VA liabilities?

	\textbf{RQ4.} How should total valuation uncertainty be decomposed into Monte Carlo,
	mortality, parameter and approximation components, and propagated to reserves, VaR
	and CTE?

	\textbf{RQ5.} How sensitive are values and predictive intervals to the choice and
	calibration of the stochastic mortality model?

	\textbf{RQ6.} Do the intervals remain reliable in tail regions, stressed markets and
	policy states that are sparse in the training design?

	\medskip
	\textbf{Objective 1.} Formulate a stochastic actuarial model for VA guarantees in
	which financial and mortality risks evolve dynamically.

	\textbf{Objective 2.} Construct a high-fidelity Monte Carlo or LSMC benchmark
	valuation engine and characterise its statistical error.

	\textbf{Objective 3.} Develop deep neural-network emulators for policy-level and
	portfolio-level liability valuation.

	\textbf{Objective 4.} Quantify uncertainty using heteroscedastic likelihoods,
	ensembles, bootstrap, Monte Carlo dropout and conformal prediction, separating the
	components of \eqref{eq:uncertainty-components} where possible.

	\textbf{Objective 5.} Establish an error decomposition and convergence or consistency
	conditions for the emulator trained on noisy Monte Carlo targets.

	\textbf{Objective 6.} Evaluate calibration, coverage, sharpness, tail reliability and
	robustness under distribution shift, and propagate uncertainty to reserves, capital
	and hedge sensitivities.

	% ============================================================
	\section{Hypotheses (Optional)} % Approx. 0.5 page

	These are falsifiable conjectures. \textbf{H1:} deep emulators reduce the cost of
	repeated VA valuation by orders of magnitude while keeping pricing error materially
	small relative to a high-fidelity benchmark. \textbf{H2:} explicitly probabilistic
	or ensemble-based networks give materially better-calibrated uncertainty than
	deterministic networks with ad hoc error bands. \textbf{H3:} stochastic mortality
	and mortality-parameter uncertainty have a non-negligible effect on predictive
	uncertainty even where their effect on the mean fee is modest. \textbf{H4:}
	tail-focused training and calibration improve reliability for high-liability policies
	and stressed states without materially sacrificing overall accuracy.

	\section{Research Design and Methodology} % Approx. 10-12 pages

	\subsection{Introduction}
	The source template frames the research design through Saunders' research onion and a
	CRISP-DM cycle, which suit applied information-systems and data-mining work. This
	proposal develops a mathematical framework and statistical guarantees and then tests
	them, so the design is stated directly and the mathematics is set out below before
	the work phases. The deviation is deliberate.

	\subsection{Research Philosophy}
	The work is quantitative and model-based. Results are derived from stated assumptions
	and then checked against controlled simulation and data, with risk-neutral pricing
	assumptions kept separate from historical estimation.

	\subsection{Approach to Theory Development}
	Deductive. Error decompositions, coverage and convergence results are derived from
	explicit assumptions and then verified numerically. Simulation supports a proof; it
	does not replace one.

	\subsection{Methodological Choice}
	Quantitative throughout: mathematical analysis, controlled simulation against
	nested Monte Carlo ground truth, and an empirical application calibrated to published
	mortality data.

	\subsection{Research Strategy}
	Construction of the benchmark engine and the emulator, followed by an uncertainty and
	coverage analysis with known data-generating laws, followed by an application limited
	to what the mortality and contract data support.

	\subsection{Time Horizon}
	Longitudinal in the empirical component: training, calibration and evaluation periods
	are separated in time so that reported coverage reflects out-of-sample behaviour.

	\subsection{Filtered Probability Space and Pricing Measure}
	Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb F,\Pp)$ with $\mathbb F=(\mathcal F_t)_{0\le t\le T}$
	be a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions, carrying the
	financial-market and mortality factors. Let $\Q$ be a risk-neutral measure for
	hedgeable financial risk. As mortality risk is generally not traded, the pricing
	measure is taken as the product of the financial risk-neutral measure and a chosen
	mortality measure under independence \citep{Biffis2005, LiLyu2026}, or more generally
	as a pricing functional carrying a mortality risk premium.

	\subsection{Financial Market Model}
	A baseline account model under $\Q$ is
	\begin{align}
		\dd S_t &= r_t S_t \dd t + \sigma_t S_t \dd W_t^{S}, \label{eq:asset}\\
		\dd A_t &= A_t\big[(r_t-\alpha)\dd t + \sigma_t \dd W_t^{S}\big] - \dd C_t,
		\label{eq:account}
	\end{align}
	where $S_t$ is the reference fund, $A_t$ the account value, $r_t$ the short rate,
	$\alpha$ the total fee rate and $C_t$ cumulative withdrawals. For stochastic
	volatility a Heston model may be used,
	\begin{align}
		\dd S_t &= r_t S_t \dd t + S_t\sqrt{V_t}\,\dd W_t^S, \\
		\dd V_t &= \kappa_V(\theta_V - V_t)\dd t + \xi\sqrt{V_t}\,\dd W_t^V,
		\qquad \dd\langle W^S,W^V\rangle_t = \rho\,\dd t,
	\end{align}
	and a rough-Heston extension replaces the variance equation by the Volterra form
	\begin{equation}
		V_t = V_0 + \int_0^t K_V(t-s)\big[\kappa_V(\theta_V-V_s)\dd s + \xi\sqrt{V_s}\,\dd W_s^V\big],
		\qquad K_V(u)=\frac{u^{H_V-1/2}}{\Gamma(H_V+1/2)},
	\end{equation}
	with $H_V\in(0,1/2)$. In the no-withdrawal case the fee-deducted account reduces to
	$A_t = S_t\exp(-\alpha t)$.

	\subsection{Stochastic Interest Rates}
	Where stochastic rates are required, a Cox-Ingersoll-Ross specification may be used,
	\begin{equation}
		\dd r_t = \kappa_r(\theta_r - r_t)\dd t + \sigma_r\sqrt{r_t}\,\dd W_t^r,
		\qquad 2\kappa_r\theta_r \ge \sigma_r^2,
	\end{equation}
	with stochastic discount factor
	\begin{equation}
		D(t,u) = \exp\!\Big(-\!\int_t^u r_s\,\dd s\Big).
		\label{eq:discount}
	\end{equation}

	\subsection{Mortality Intensity and Survival}
	Let $\tau_x$ be the future lifetime of a policyholder aged $x$ and $\mu_{x+t}$ the
	stochastic force of mortality. Conditional on the mortality filtration $\mathcal G_t$,
	\begin{equation}
		{}_{u-t}p_{x+t} = \Pp(\tau_x>u\mid\mathcal G_t)
		= \E\!\left[\exp\!\Big(-\!\int_t^u \mu_{x+s}\,\dd s\Big)\,\Big|\,\mathcal G_t\right].
		\label{eq:survival}
	\end{equation}
	A tractable baseline diffusion is
	\begin{equation}
		\dd\mu_t = \kappa_\mu(\theta_\mu - \mu_t)\dd t + \sigma_\mu\,g(\mu_t)\,\dd W_t^\mu,
		\label{eq:mortality-diffusion}
	\end{equation}
	with $g$ chosen to preserve positivity. A more advanced specification with long-range
	dependence \citep{Wang2021, LiLyu2026} is the Volterra mortality model
	\begin{equation}
		\mu_{x+t} = \mu_x + \int_0^t K_\mu(t-u)\,b(\mu_{x+u})\dd u
		+ \int_0^t K_\mu(t-u)\,\sigma(\mu_{x+u})\dd B_u,
		\qquad K_\mu(u)=\frac{u^{H_\mu-1/2}}{\Gamma(H_\mu+1/2)},
		\label{eq:volterra}
	\end{equation}
	for which $H_\mu>1/2$ induces long-range dependence and $H_\mu=1/2$ recovers the
	Markovian limit. On a grid $0=t_0<\cdots<t_N$, survival along a simulated path is
	approximated by the trapezoidal rule,
	\begin{equation}
		{}_{t_n}p_x^{(m)} \approx \exp\!\Big\{-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
		\big(\mu_{x+t_k}^{(m)} + \mu_{x+t_{k+1}}^{(m)}\big)\Big\}.
	\end{equation}

	\subsection{Mortality Calibration and Parameter Uncertainty}
	Given observed cohort survival proportions $\ell_{x+s}/\ell_x$, parameters
	$\vartheta_\mu$ are estimated by nonlinear least squares,
	\begin{equation}
		\widehat\vartheta_\mu = \argmin_{\vartheta_\mu\in\Theta_\mu}
		\frac1N\sum_{s=1}^{N}\Big[\,{}_sp_x(\vartheta_\mu) - \frac{\ell_{x+s}}{\ell_x}\Big]^2,
	\end{equation}
	and parameter uncertainty is studied by nonparametric or parametric bootstrap or by
	Bayesian posterior sampling \citep{CairnsBlakeDowd2006}.

	\subsection{Guaranteed Benefits and Net Liability}
	For a maturity guarantee $G_T$, the gross GMMB liability is
	\begin{equation}
		L_T^{\mathrm{GMMB}} = \ind_{\{\tau_x>T\}}\,D(0,T)\,(G_T - A_T)^+,
	\end{equation}
	and for a death benefit $G^D$,
	\begin{equation}
		L^{\mathrm{GMDB}} = \ind_{\{\tau_x\le T\}}\,D(0,\tau_x)\,(G^D_{\tau_x} - A_{\tau_x})^+ .
	\end{equation}
	A combined guarantee is $H = L_T^{\mathrm{GMMB}} + L^{\mathrm{GMDB}}$. If fees at rate
	$\alpha_g$ fund the guarantee, the discounted fee income is
	$I = \alpha_g\int_0^{T\wedge\tau_x} D(0,t)\,A_t\,\dd t$, and the net liability is
	\begin{equation}
		L = H - I.
		\label{eq:net-liability}
	\end{equation}
	The gross-versus-net distinction matters because adverse markets raise guarantee costs
	and reduce fee income simultaneously \citep{FengYi2019}.

	\subsection{Market-Consistent Valuation and the Fair Fee}
	The fair value is $V_0 = \E^{\Q}[L\mid\mathcal F_0]$, and the fair fee $c^\star$
	solves $U_0(c^\star)=A_0$, where $U_0(c)$ is the value of the full contract under fee
	$c$; monotonicity of $U_0$ permits a bisection solution.

	\subsection{Optimal Stopping and Stochastic Control}
	Contracts with surrender or dynamic withdrawals are formulated on a decision grid
	$0=t_0<\cdots<t_N=T$ with state $X_n$ and control $\pi_n\in\mathcal A_n(X_n)$ and
	controlled transition $X_{n+1}=\mathcal T_n(X_n,\pi_n,Z_{n+1})$. Writing
	$\beta_{n,n+1}=D(t_n,t_{n+1})$, the value function satisfies the Bellman recursion
	\begin{equation}
		V_n(x) = \sup_{\pi\in\mathcal A_n(x)}\Big\{ R_n(x,\pi)
		+ \E^{\Q}\!\big[\beta_{n,n+1}V_{n+1}(X_{n+1})\mid X_n=x,\pi_n=\pi\big]\Big\},
		\qquad V_N(x)=R_N(x).
		\label{eq:bellman}
	\end{equation}
	The continuation function
	$\Phi_n(x,\pi)=\E^{\Q}[\beta_{n,n+1}V_{n+1}(X_{n+1})\mid X_n=x,\pi_n=\pi]$ is
	approximated by least-squares Monte Carlo \citep{LongstaffSchwartz2001, LuoShevchenko2015},
	\begin{equation}
		\beta_{n,n+1}V_{n+1}(X_{n+1}^{(m)}) = f_{\theta_n}(X_n^{(m)},\pi_n^{(m)}) + \varepsilon_n^{(m)},
	\end{equation}
	with the regression function $f_{\theta_n}$ taken to be a neural network in high
	dimensions. For pure surrender with payoff $g_n$, the recursion is
	$V_n(X_n)=\max\{g_n(X_n),\Phi_n(X_n)\}$ with optimal stopping time
	$\tau^\star=\inf\{t_n: g_n(X_n)\ge\Phi_n(X_n)\}$.

	\subsection{Monte Carlo Valuation and Statistical Error}
	For a state $x$ the benchmark estimator is \eqref{eq:mc-estimator}, and conditional on
	$X=x$,
	\begin{equation}
		\E[\widehat V_M(x)\mid x] = V(x),
		\qquad
		\Var(\widehat V_M(x)\mid x) = \frac{\sigma_H^2(x)}{M},
		\qquad \sigma_H^2(x)=\Var(H(x,\omega)\mid X=x),
		\label{eq:mc-var}
	\end{equation}
	with empirical standard error
	\begin{equation}
		\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}_{\mathrm{MC}}(x)
		= \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{M(M-1)}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\big(H^{(m)}(x)-\widehat V_M(x)\big)^2}.
		\label{eq:mc-se}
	\end{equation}
	The supervised response is therefore heteroscedastic across states, and under
	finite-variance conditions
	$\sqrt{M}(\widehat V_M(x)-V(x))\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal N(0,\sigma_H^2(x))$. For nested
	simulation with outer states $Z_i$ and inner draws $Y_{ij}$,
	$\widehat V_{M,N}=\tfrac1N\sum_i(\tfrac1M\sum_j Y_{ij})$, and the project studies
	optimal allocation under a fixed budget $B\approx NM$. Antithetic variates, control
	variates, common random numbers and quasi-Monte Carlo will be used; for a control $C$,
	$\widehat V_{\mathrm{CV}}=\widehat V_M - b(\overline C_M-\E[C])$ with
	$b^\star=\Cov(H,C)/\Var(C)$.

	\subsection{Deep Neural Network Emulator}
	Let $x\in\R^p$ be the transformed state. A feedforward network with $L$ hidden layers
	is
	\begin{equation}
		h^{(0)}=x,\quad h^{(\ell)}=\phi_\ell\big(W^{(\ell)}h^{(\ell-1)}+b^{(\ell)}\big),\ \ell=1,\dots,L,
		\quad f_\theta(x)=W^{(L+1)}h^{(L)}+b^{(L+1)},
		\label{eq:dnn}
	\end{equation}
	trained by the weighted empirical risk
	\begin{equation}
		\widehat\theta = \argmin_\theta \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}
		w_i\big(\widehat V_{M_i}(x_i)-f_\theta(x_i)\big)^2 + \lambda\,\mathcal R(\theta),
		\label{eq:nn-loss}
	\end{equation}
	where tail liabilities may be upweighted and $\mathcal R$ is a regulariser. When Monte
	Carlo standard errors are available, an efficient choice is
	$w_i\propto\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}_{\mathrm{MC}}(x_i)^{-2}$, with small estimated errors
	stabilised. Writing $f^\star=V$ and
	$f_{\mathcal F}^\star=\argmin_{f\in\mathcal F}\E[(V(X)-f(X))^2]$, the error splits as
	\begin{equation}
		\widehat f(x)-f^\star(x)
		= \underbrace{\big(\widehat f(x)-f_{\mathcal F}^\star(x)\big)}_{\text{estimation/optimisation}}
		+ \underbrace{\big(f_{\mathcal F}^\star(x)-f^\star(x)\big)}_{\text{approximation}},
	\end{equation}
	with the observed response carrying additional Monte Carlo noise
	$\widehat V_M(x)=V(x)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{MC}}(x)$, $\E[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{MC}}(x)\mid x]=0$.

	\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification}
	For predictive parameter $\Theta$ and data $\mathcal D$, the law of total variance gives
	\begin{equation}
		\Var(Y^\star\mid x,\mathcal D)
		= \underbrace{\E_{\Theta\mid\mathcal D}\big[\Var(Y^\star\mid x,\Theta)\big]}_{\text{aleatoric}}
		+ \underbrace{\Var_{\Theta\mid\mathcal D}\big[\E(Y^\star\mid x,\Theta)\big]}_{\text{epistemic}},
		\label{eq:total-variance}
	\end{equation}
	with the aleatoric term refined to separate intrinsic path variation from finite Monte
	Carlo target noise. A Gaussian working model
	$Y_i\mid X_i=x_i\sim\mathcal N(\mu_\theta(x_i),\sigma_\theta^2(x_i))$ is trained by the
	heteroscedastic negative log-likelihood
	\begin{equation}
		\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NLL}}(\theta)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}
		\Big[\frac{(y_i-\mu_\theta(x_i))^2}{2\sigma_\theta^2(x_i)}+\tfrac12\log\sigma_\theta^2(x_i)\Big],
		\qquad \sigma_\theta(x)=\log(1+e^{s_\theta(x)})+\epsilon,
		\label{eq:hetero-nll}
	\end{equation}
	with a Student-$t$ likelihood considered for heavy-tailed residuals
	\citep{KendallGal2017}. A deep ensemble of $K$ networks \citep{Lakshminarayanan2017}
	gives mean $\overline\mu(x)=\tfrac1K\sum_k\mu_k(x)$ and predictive variance
	\begin{equation}
		\widehat\sigma^2_{\mathrm{ens}}(x)=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sigma_k^2(x)
		+\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mu_k^2(x)-\overline\mu^2(x),
		\label{eq:ensemble-var}
	\end{equation}
	and Monte-Carlo dropout \citep{GalGhahramani2016} and bootstrap networks provide
	complementary estimates, the bootstrap also resampling mortality parameters or data to
	propagate $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{param}}$ and $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{mort}}$. For
	distribution-free coverage, split-conformal calibration \citep{Lei2018} uses residuals
	$r_i=|y_i-\widehat f(x_i)|$ and a quantile $\widehat q_{1-\alpha}$ to form
	$C_{1-\alpha}(x)=[\widehat f(x)-\widehat q_{1-\alpha},\widehat f(x)+\widehat q_{1-\alpha}]$,
	while conformalized quantile regression \citep{RomanoEtAl2019} calibrates estimated
	conditional quantiles with scores
	$R_i=\max\{\widehat q_{\alpha/2}(x_i)-y_i,\ y_i-\widehat q_{1-\alpha/2}(x_i)\}$ to adapt
	interval width to heteroscedasticity. A structural point is that exchangeability holds
	by construction here, because the design states are sampled independently, in contrast
	to the dependent time-series setting.

	\subsection{Error Decomposition and Convergence Targets}
	A central theoretical objective is to establish an error decomposition of the form
	\begin{equation}
		\big|\widehat V_{\mathrm{final}}(x)-V(x)\big|
		\le E_{\mathrm{MC}}(x) + E_{\mathrm{time}}(x) + E_{\mathrm{mort}}(x)
		+ E_{\mathrm{approx}}(x) + E_{\mathrm{est}}(x) + E_{\mathrm{opt}}(x),
		\label{eq:master-error}
	\end{equation}
	whose terms are Monte Carlo sampling, time discretisation, mortality discretisation or
	calibration, neural-network approximation, statistical estimation and numerical
	optimisation error; a truncated-signature extension adds a term $E_{\mathrm{sig}}(K)$.
	The target result gives conditions under which the controllable terms vanish as
	simulation size, time resolution, signature depth and network capacity increase in a
	controlled sequence, yielding $L^2(P_X)$ consistency
	\begin{equation}
		\int \big|\widehat f_n(x)-V(x)\big|^2\,\dd P_X(x)\xrightarrow{P}0
		\label{eq:l2-consistency}
	\end{equation}
	under compactness or tail control of $X$, universal approximation of the network class,
	vanishing optimisation error and increasing simulation accuracy.

	\subsection{Actuarial Risk Measures and Hedging Sensitivities}
	For the runoff net liability $L$ and level $\alpha\in(0,1)$,
	\begin{equation}
		\VaR_\alpha(L)=\inf\{\ell\in\R:\Pp(L\le\ell)\ge\alpha\},
		\qquad
		\CTE_\alpha(L)=\E[L\mid L>\VaR_\alpha(L)],
		\label{eq:var-cte}
	\end{equation}
	and the effect of emulator uncertainty is studied through
	$\Delta_{\mathrm{VaR}}=\VaR_\alpha(\widehat L_{\mathrm{NN}})-\VaR_\alpha(L)$ and its CTE
	analogue across simulation replicates and stresses. For a differentiable emulator,
	automatic differentiation yields hedge sensitivities
	$\Delta_{\mathrm{NN}}=\partial f_\theta/\partial S$,
	$\Gamma_{\mathrm{NN}}=\partial^2 f_\theta/\partial S^2$,
	$\nu_{\mathrm{NN}}=\partial f_\theta/\partial\sigma$ and a mortality sensitivity
	$\mathcal M_{\mathrm{NN}}=\partial f_\theta/\partial\mu$, benchmarked against
	finite-difference Monte Carlo estimators with common random numbers.

	\subsection{Experimental Design and Evaluation}
	The state vector is
	$X=(A_0,G_0,x,T,\alpha,r_0,V_0,\mu_x,\vartheta_\mu,\vartheta_{\mathrm{mkt}})$, and design
	points are drawn by Latin hypercube or space-filling designs, with density augmented
	near $G_0/A_0\approx 1$ and in stressed regions. The dataset is
	$\mathcal D=\{(x_i,\widehat V_{M_i}(x_i),\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}_{\mathrm{MC}}(x_i))\}_{i=1}^{n}$,
	with replicated valuations at selected states to validate the target-noise model. Point
	accuracy is measured by MAE, RMSE and (where denominators are bounded away from zero)
	MAPE; interval quality by empirical coverage
	$\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}_{1-\alpha}=\tfrac1n\sum_i\ind\{L_i\le y_i\le U_i\}$, mean width,
	and, conditionally, across strata of age, moneyness, volatility, mortality intensity
	and liability quantile; and probabilistic accuracy by the negative log score and the
	continuous ranked probability score \citep{GneitingRaftery2007}
	\begin{equation}
		\mathrm{CRPS}(F,y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(F(z)-\ind\{y\le z\}\big)^2\,\dd z .
	\end{equation}
	Under distribution shift from $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ to $P_{\mathrm{stress}}$,
	degradation is $\Delta_{\mathrm{shift}}=\mathcal R_{P_{\mathrm{stress}}}(\widehat f)-\mathcal R_{P_{\mathrm{train}}}(\widehat f)$,
	and a hybrid fallback uses full simulation when
	$\widehat\sigma_{\mathrm{pred}}(x)>c_{\mathrm{UQ}}$ or
	$d(x,\mathcal X_{\mathrm{train}})>c_d$, so the emulator prices ordinary states while the
	stochastic engine is retained for uncertain ones.

	\section{Validity and Reliability} % Approx. 0.5 page

	\subsection{Validity}
	Accuracy is judged against nested Monte Carlo ground truth rather than the emulator's
	own labels; coverage statements are made explicitly marginal or conditional; and tail
	strata are reported separately because overall RMSE can conceal poor performance where
	insurer losses are largest.

	\subsection{Reliability}
	All simulation designs, parameter configurations and random-number seeds are version
	controlled, Monte Carlo error is reported on every number, paired random numbers reduce
	the variance of method comparisons, and notebooks regenerate each figure and table.

	\section{Ethical Considerations} % Approx. 0.5 page

	The project uses synthetic policy portfolios and public mortality data (the Human
	Mortality Database and, where applicable, published South African tables), avoiding
	identifiable policyholder information, so no participant consent arises. If proprietary
	data are later introduced, institutional ethics and data-governance requirements of the
	University of the Free State will be followed.

	\section{Expected Contributions of the Research} % Approx. 1 page

	\subsection{Mathematical Contributions}
	A formal stochastic representation of the VA net liability under joint financial and
	stochastic mortality risk; the error decomposition \eqref{eq:master-error} and
	consistency conditions \eqref{eq:l2-consistency} for an emulator trained on noisy Monte
	Carlo targets; calibration results for conformal and probabilistic intervals under
	heteroscedastic simulation noise; a justified allocation of computational budget between
	design points and inner paths; stability results for emulator-derived hedge
	sensitivities; and a path-dependent extension combining neural regression with
	signatures for non-Markovian mortality and volatility. Results are labelled proposed
	until established.

	\subsection{Actuarial and Practical Contributions}
	Faster policy-level and portfolio-level valuation; explicit uncertainty intervals around
	reserves and liabilities; quantified mortality-model risk; improved tail diagnostics;
	uncertainty-aware VaR and CTE; more stable hedge sensitivities; and a practical rule for
	when a surrogate should defer to full stochastic simulation, together with an
	open-source implementation and reproducible benchmarks.

	% ============================================================
	\section{Limitations of the Study} % Approx. 0.5 page

	Conformal guarantees are marginal rather than conditional and hold under exchangeability
	of the sampled states; departures from the assumed data-generating model, the restriction
	to a chosen contract family, and the quality of the mortality calibration all bound what
	can be claimed. The emulator inherits any bias of the nested-simulation labels, and the
	South African application is constrained by data availability.
