## Abstract 〔~0.5 pg〕
- 150–250 words. Name: the product (variable-annuity guarantees), the computational pain
  (nested Monte Carlo under stochastic mortality), the method (a DNN **emulator** mapping a
  state vector to the present value of the guarantee liability), and the headline contribution
  (the emulator reports not just a point value but a **calibrated predictive interval**, with the
  epistemic/aleatoric split and finite-sample coverage established).

## 1. Introduction and Background 〔~1.5 pg〕
- 1.1 Variable annuities and their embedded guarantees (GMAB/GMDB/GMWB); why insurers must
  revalue a large book daily for pricing, hedging and capital.
- 1.2 **Nested Monte Carlo**: outer real-world scenarios × inner risk-neutral valuation paths;
  how **stochastic mortality** enlarges the state and makes it prohibitive.
- 1.3 **Emulation / surrogate modelling** as the remedy: learn the valuation map once, evaluate
  instantly.
- 1.4 Why a point value is not enough: capital and model-risk decisions need **quantified
  uncertainty** on the emulated value.
- 1.5 Positioning vs. the candidate's MSc (deep hedging / DNN valuation + SHAP interpretation):
  this PhD reuses the deep-learning craft but turns to **emulation + uncertainty quantification**
  under stochastic mortality.

## 2. Problem Statement and Research Gap 〔~1 pg〕
- 2.1 What exists: NN emulators for VA portfolios — Hejazi & Jackson (2016, 2017), Gan & Lin
  (2015, 2018); UQ methods — deep ensembles, Bayesian NN, conformal prediction.
- 2.2 **The gap:** existing VA emulators return **point** values, use mostly **deterministic or
  static** mortality, and carry **no calibrated uncertainty**. No work delivers a VA-guarantee
  emulator that (i) ingests a **stochastic-mortality** state and (ii) returns a **coverage-
  guaranteed predictive interval** separating emulator error from financial/mortality risk.
- 2.3 One-sentence statement of the problem this thesis closes.

## 3. Research Questions and Objectives 〔~0.75 pg〕
- **RQ1.** Can a DNN emulate the present value of a VA guarantee liability, as a function of the
  full policy/financial/mortality state vector, to actuarial accuracy against nested-MC targets?
- **RQ2.** How should the emulator's **predictive uncertainty** be represented and **decomposed**
  into epistemic (emulator) and aleatoric (financial + mortality) components?
- **RQ3.** Can **finite-sample coverage** of the predictive intervals be guaranteed (e.g. via
  split-conformal prediction) under the dependence present in the training labels?
- **RQ4.** What is the impact of the quantified uncertainty on **reserves/capital**, on real data?
- **Objectives** — bulleted, mapped 1:1 to the RQs and to §7.

## 5. Model Specification 〔~1.5 pg〕
- 5.1 **The emulator map.** Learn `f_theta : x -> Y`, where `Y` is the present value of the
  guarantee liability and `x` is the state vector below. Target labels generated by nested
  Monte Carlo under a stochastic-mortality model.
- 5.2 **Input vector `x`** (three blocks):
  - *Policy characteristics:* policyholder age; sex (if in the mortality basis, handled
    appropriately); contract duration; initial premium; current account value; guarantee base;
    time to maturity; surrender-charge schedule; withdrawal rate; guarantee type.
  - *Financial-state variables:* interest rate; equity level / moneyness; equity volatility;
    stochastic-volatility state; correlation parameters; fee rate.
  - *Mortality variables:* current mortality intensity; mortality trend; mortality volatility;
    cohort effect; financial–mortality correlation; mortality-model parameters.
- 5.3 **Output.** A single continuous response `Y = present value of the guarantee liability`,
  **and** a calibrated predictive interval `Y ∈ [L(x), U(x)]` with coverage `≥ 1 − α`.
- 5.4 **Stochastic-mortality setup.** Affine intensity `μ_t` (Biffis-type) / Lee–Carter dynamics;
  financial–mortality correlation; how mortality scenarios enter both the labels and `x`.
- 5.5 **Uncertainty model.** Predictive distribution of `Y | x`; epistemic (parameter/emulator)
  vs. aleatoric (simulation/mortality) decomposition.

## 6. Methodology 〔~1 pg〕
- 6.1 **Emulation track:** nested-MC label generation; feature engineering of `x`; architecture
  (FNN; GRU where path state matters); training, regularisation, hyperparameter search (Optuna).
- 6.2 **UQ track:** deep ensembles / Bayesian last-layer / MC-dropout for epistemic; a learned
  variance head for aleatoric; **split-conformal** wrapping for distribution-free coverage;
  calibration diagnostics (empirical coverage, mean interval width, CRPS, reliability curves).
- 6.3 **Validation track:** benchmark value and intervals vs. full nested MC; stress by
  moneyness, age, time-to-maturity and mortality regime; capital/reserve impact study.

## 7. Statistical Contributions (to be established) 〔~0.75 pg〕
- **C1.** A DNN emulator for VA-guarantee PV under stochastic mortality, with documented accuracy
  vs. nested MC.
- **C2.** A principled **epistemic/aleatoric decomposition** of the emulator's predictive
  uncertainty for this valuation problem.
- **C3.** **Finite-sample coverage guarantee** for the predictive intervals (split-conformal),
  accounting for dependence in the labels — the doctoral contribution.
- **C4.** (Stretch) Error/sample-complexity bounds linking label budget, network capacity and
  interval width.
